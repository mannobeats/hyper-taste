# Words

Copy is where "generated" shows first, and on a page that sells it's half the design. Most of
the fix is writing less, plainly, about this one product.

## Headlines

- **Claims from the visitor's world, not slogans.** "Good design leaves a mark." could sell
  anything. "Your neighbour's dog will not be on this episode." sells one audio tool to one
  podcaster. Use a noun, a number or a situation only this product owns.
- **Write five, keep one:** a plain statement, a number, a question, a promise, a scene. Keep
  the one true only of this product that still makes someone look twice.
- **Two tones:** a claim, then a quieter clause that finishes it ("Pay for minutes of audio.
  Nothing else."). The second half reassures, narrows or turns the first.
- **Vary the shape across the page:** statements, questions, a number, the two-tone pair, short
  titles with a turn ("The room, gone."). Not every headline in one shape.
- **The line under a headline** says how and for whom, in one sentence.
- **Small lines count:** captions, notes, the footer. A line with no fact gets cut.

## Captions that tell a story

A film's captions read as one story when read alone, one step each, present tense: "Drop in any
recording." "Your engineer listens to all of it first." "The room falls away. You stay." "Ready
for anywhere you post."

## Plain, specific, human

Never: elevate, seamless, unleash, revolutionize, next-gen, cutting-edge, game-changer, empower,
supercharge, unlock, robust, leverage, delve, curated, crafted, "in today's fast-paced world",
"take your X to the next level", "all-in-one platform".

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| Revolutionize your invoicing workflow | Get paid without the awkward follow-up |
| Leverage AI-powered insights | See which clients pay late |
| Oops! Something went wrong. | We couldn't save your changes. Try again. |
| Success! Your changes have been saved! | Changes saved |
| Are you sure? Yes / No | Delete "Demo"? It can't be undone. Cancel / Delete project |

- **Translate the jargon** into what people hear or see: "Quiet gaps" for a noise gate, "Sharp
  S's" for a de-esser, "Right level" for −14 LUFS.
- **Buttons** name the action in 1–3 words ("Try it on your voice", "Send invoice"). Never
  "Submit", "Click here" or "Learn more" alone. One label per intent, nav included.
- **Don't repeat what's on screen.** Under "Votes" the list is "Last 10", not "Last 10 votes".
- **Say what clicking costs** beside the button, and keep promises true ("Never used to train
  AI" only if it never is).
- Friendly on marketing pages; precise inside the app.

## Casing and punctuation

- Sentence case everywhere. Labels are nouns ("Revenue"). Steps are named with their verb
  ("Connect"), not "Step 03".
- Periods and commas; headlines may end with a period. No exclamation marks on success, no long
  dash-joined sentences, typographic quotes (“ ”) and apostrophes (’).

## States

- **Empty:** one line on what goes here, plus the button that fills it.
- **Errors:** what happened and what to do, active voice, next to the field.
- **Destructive:** what will be lost ("Its 14 files are removed for everyone").

## One sample world

Placeholder content makes a design look fake before anyone reads it. Invent one world and run it
through every screen: one person with a name local to the product's place, their situation, and
their data ("Rosa Delgado, Episode 12, kitchen table, 0:38"). The same take in the hero, the
cards and the pricing example.

- Names varied and believable; never "John Doe" or "Acme".
- Numbers plausible and varied ($1,850.00, 12 days, 3.2 sessions), one format per kind.
- Dates in a sensible order relative to today.
- Quotes and customer names only when the user gave them; otherwise a labeled slot. Invented
  numbers on a real product are listed for the user to confirm.
