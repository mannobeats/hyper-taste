# Landing: pages that sell

For landing, launch, product, pricing, portfolio and event pages. The layout, order, palette and
type come from this product every time; the finish comes from `craft.md`.

## The direction block

Write it into `DESIGN.md` before any code, and say it in one short paragraph in your reply.

```
Product and visitor: <what it is, for whom>. They arrive asking: <their question>.
Action: "<button, 1–3 words>", and the line beside it that lowers the risk.
Obvious version: <the page any model would build: first screen, order, palette, type>.
Blueprint: <one site at the bar you want>. Take: <its film, materials, type, motion…>.
  Ours: <the content, the accent, the signature>.
Directions, each from one thing in the product's world (an object, a place, a ritual, a material):
  A. <the thing>: <first screen> / <order> / <palette> / <type>
  B. …
  C. …
Pick: <letter>, because <one sentence>.
Signature: "It's the site where <what happens to the visitor>", built as <a product film | a scroll
  scene | something to try | a photograph | type as the image>.
Grammar: <from the table below>. Curve: one line per section, the feeling then what on screen
  causes it. Peak: <which>.
Palette: <2–4 colors> from <where in the product's world>. Leads <light | dark>, because <reason>.
Type: <family>, because <reason>. Display <size> at 1440.
Images: <each one: its job and its source: the real UI, the user, generated, drawn>.
Sample world: <one person and one situation every screen shows>.
First screen: <one sentence no other product could claim>.
```

## Fill it

- **Start from the product's world, not its category.** List the objects, places, rituals,
  materials and moments around it. A climbing gym has the route wall, chalk and the logbook; an
  audio tool has the take, the room noise and the meter. The idea is one of these, and it
  decides the first screen, the palette and the type.
- **The obvious version** is the category's usual page plus the list in `SKILL.md`. Write it
  down so you can see when you drift back to it.
- **The blueprint** is one reference at the quality you want, from the user or the best site in
  a neighboring field. Take its craft (how it shows the product, its materials, its type, its
  motion) and translate it into this product's tokens. Its content, words and logo stay
  behind. One blueprint keeps a page consistent; five make it a collage.
- **Three directions that differ in structure:** where the words sit, how big the visual is,
  what the eye meets first. Then pick, and turn the pick up: bigger scale, a stronger idea, more
  of the product, and pull back only what hurts reading or speed.
- **The signature** is the one thing only this product could show: its core action playing, a
  surprising number, a photograph, a control to try. It lives on the first screen or is the
  first thing you scroll into, and it gets the most space and care. If the page would fit
  another product unchanged, keep looking.
- **What worked in testing:** a headline in the visitor's own situation ("Your neighbour's dog
  will not be on this episode."), the product's real window playing its job in chapters under
  it, a booking form that was the headline ("Board in [Amsterdam] after dinner"), a drawn
  section of a quay wall that clears as you scroll. What failed: a strong idea placed below a
  safe first screen, and a scroll story people lost when they skimmed back.

## Grammar

What the page is: what a section is, what moves it, how it ends. Pick one and keep its rules.

| Grammar | Fits | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| Live product | Software | The product window is the hero and plays its job as a film; every later picture is a real state of the product (`craft.md`) |
| Scroll scene | Physical products, places, launches | A pinned or layered scene the scroll moves through, then calm sections (`visuals.md`) |
| Editorial | A method, a story, research | Large type, a measured column, photographs with one grade, the action as running text |
| Poster | An event, a manifesto, a strong idea | Type is the image; sections change scale, not media |
| Gallery | Work, places, collections | Images lead, captions small, an index or a filter |
| Split stage | Two things in dialogue (before and after) | The stage fills the first screen, then holds one side while the story scrolls beside it |

Software starts from live product. People skim back and forth, so a story that only works
scrolled forward at the right speed gets lost; a film with its own clock and pickable steps
survives skimming.

**The curve:** two neighbors with the same feeling means one is filler. One peak, with the best
asset and a quieter section before it. Specs, pricing and questions are short ordinary
sections. The ending resolves and holds on content.

## The first screen

A short concrete headline, at most one supporting sentence, one primary action with its risk
line plus at most one secondary, and the visual or its clear start. On a 1280×720 laptop all of
these show. Decide what carries the screen:

- **The product itself,** large and working: the live product grammar.
- **A photograph or scene** for places, food and physical things, the words on its calm area.
- **Something to try:** type, record, drag, toggle, right there.
- **A statement:** type alone, the visual arriving on the first scroll.
- **The data:** one number, a live list, a map.

Then place the words: centered over or above the visual, anchored to a bottom corner of a
full-bleed image, running full width with the product breaking into it, or after the visual.

Whatever carries it, the first screen stands on a backdrop with depth and light: a photograph
from the product's world, or the brand material as a live field (`craft.md`). A flat white or
black first screen with a product shot under the headline is the obvious version.

## Order by the visitor's questions

Order the sections by what this visitor asks next, and drop what they don't ask:

- software: what it does (the film), try it, what else it does (product states), why trust it,
  what it costs, questions about limits
- a café: today's menu and the map; an event: when, where, who, how much
- hardware: what it sees or makes, then the object, then the specs one line at a time
- a portfolio: the work before the studio

Each section gets its own form: a full-bleed image, a live piece of product, a list, one huge
number, a table, a quiet pause. Never the same composition three times in a row.

## Make it convert

- **The headline says what it is and what the visitor gets;** the line under it says how and
  for whom.
- **One action, one label, everywhere,** nav included, coming back after each big proof. On
  long pages the nav keeps it in reach.
- **Say what clicking costs** in one quiet line by the button ("10 free minutes, no account").
- **Let them try it on the page** when the product allows: record, upload, paste, pick a date.
  It beats any claim.
- **Proof that is real:** named customers, real numbers, a quote with a name and a detail, or
  facts about the product ("40 MB", "no account needed"). Mark sample data.
- **Questions** answer real limits (what's free, what happens to my data, how cancelling works),
  not filler.

## Pricing

Two or three plans. The recommended one gets the darker button or a firmer edge, not a taller
card, a glow or a "Most popular" pill. One line of what's different per plan, 3–5 concrete
items each, what's free and how billing works.

## Finishing

- **Footer:** the closing line and action, a few links, legal. The name drawn large is a good
  last frame (`craft.md`).
- **Meta:** title, description, Open Graph image, favicon. **404** in the product's voice with a
  way back.
- **Analytics** on live sites: tag the primary button and the form, trackers after consent.
